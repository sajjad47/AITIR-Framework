Adaptive Identity-and-access Threat Intelligence and Response

A vendor-neutral architecture for converting identity-centered telemetry into provenance-linked evidence, a bounded policy decision, an authorized response, and independently reviewable assurance.

Core rule: Evidence in, authority out. Analytics produce evidence. Authorized policy decides. Enforcement acts. Assurance verifies.

This exhibit describes a reference architecture and research preview. It is not software, a production deployment, compliance certification, agency endorsement, legal determination, or evidence of operational effectiveness.

1 · Executive Technical Summary

Identity security decisions combine uncertain telemetry, organizational policy, mission impact, intervention cost, and authority. AITIR 2.0 prevents these concerns from collapsing into one opaque “AI risk score.” It separates events, analytical evidence, policy decisions, enforcement, outcome verification, feedback, and assurance.

Reference state machine

proposed → denied proposed → authorized → executing → succeeded └────────→ failed → new evidence + new decision succeeded / failed → separately authorized rollback stale proposed / authorized → expired (cannot execute)

Twelve guard categories cover risk basis, freshness, corroboration, approval, separation of duty, rollback, critical targets, blast radius, holds, refresh after failure, aging evidence, and break-glass scope.

Enforcement rule: A connector rechecks decision validity and target preconditions, uses an idempotency key, preserves evidence, records partial failure, and verifies the resulting state independently where feasible.

5 · Evidence Boundary and Responsible Use

Checked repository evidence

The 12-row synthetic example now reconciles identifiers, feature points, tiers, routing, and counts. The distribution is High 3, Medium 8, Low 1. The historical narrative discrepancy is corrected and automatically checked.

It cannot estimate accuracy, precision, recall, false-positive rate, calibration, fairness, safety, operational latency, incident reduction, analyst workload, or financial benefit. It has no ground-truth attack labels.

Known limitations

  • Identity resolution can merge or split subjects incorrectly.
  • Anomalies can be benign; calibrated probabilities decay under shift.
  • Threat feeds, graph edges, explanations, labels, and feedback can be wrong or manipulated.
  • Connectors can race, partially fail, duplicate action, or report premature success.
  • Generative AI can be manipulated by untrusted log and threat-feed text.
  • Consequential actions involve legal, privacy, labor, accessibility, evidence, records, and continuity duties outside a model.

Responsible-use conditions

  1. Obtain local authority and multidisciplinary review.
  2. Keep analytics outside the enforcement trust root.
  3. Begin with architecture, offline replay, shadow, and advisory stages.
  4. Measure abstention queue capacity and human-review error.
  5. Test policy, state, expiration, replay, idempotency, failure, rollback, and degraded modes.
  6. Use claims only within the scope of collected evidence.
